## Corrections Policy

**Last updated:** September 25, 2026

We get things wrong sometimes. Here is exactly what we do about it.

### How to tell us

Use the [Contact](/contact/) page and include:

1. the article URL,
2. the specific sentence or figure you think is wrong, and
3. a link to your source, if you have one.

You do not need an account, and we do not require anything else from you.

### What happens next

- **We acknowledge** substantive reports within 7 days.
- **We check the claim** against the original source material, not against another blog.
- **If we are wrong, we fix it** — typically within 14 days of confirmation, sooner if the error could affect a pet's health or safety or cause someone to spend money wrongly. Health- or safety-relevant errors jump the queue.
- **If we are right**, we reply and explain why, with the source.

### How corrections are shown

- **Factual corrections:** we edit the article and add a dated note directly under the corrected passage (or at the foot of the article for small fixes), saying what changed — e.g. *"Correction (September 25, 2026): the serving size was previously stated as 2 chews; the label says 1."* We do not quietly rewrite history.
- **Material changes** (a recommendation changes, a product leaves the list, a claim is removed) get an "**Updated:**" note with the date.
- **Typos, formatting, and broken links** are fixed silently — no note needed for a comma.

### Routine updates

Content is also refreshed on a schedule, not only when someone complains. Published articles carry dated "Updated" notes, so you can always see how recent the advice is.

### What we will not do

- Remove an article to hide an error (it gets corrected and annotated instead).
- Edit a substantive passage without saying so.
- Delay a health- or safety-relevant fix for editorial convenience.

### Escalation

If a report is disputed after our first reply, ask for a second review via [Contact](/contact/) — a second editor will re-check the source material. For health claims, we will also ask a qualified veterinarian to look at it where the question is clinical.
